Ledger review

Disclosure: the button below may become an affiliate link. If you sign up through it we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. That doesn't change what we write. No star ratings: we don't publish scores we can't back with testing data. Details and prices change, so check the official site.

Ledger is a French company that makes hardware wallets (such as the Nano S Plus, Nano X, Flex and Stax) which keep your private keys on a device with a secure element chip. You manage coins through the Ledger Live app and confirm every transaction on the device.

Good for

  • Holding meaningful amounts of crypto long-term
  • Separating private keys from an internet-connected computer/phone
  • Using many chains from one device

Skip it if

  • People who won't safely store a 24-word recovery phrase
  • Very small balances where the device cost doesn't make sense

Pros

  • Keys stay on the device; transactions are confirmed on-device
  • Supports a large number of coins and tokens
  • Ledger Live app for portfolio and app management
  • Works with many third-party wallets

Cons

  • 2020 customer data breach leaked customer emails/addresses (phishing risk followed)
  • 2023 'Ledger Recover' (optional seed backup service) drew criticism over firmware trust
  • Dec 2023 Connect Kit library supply-chain attack affected some dApps
  • Firmware is not fully open source

Pricing

One-time device purchase; models differ in price, screen and connectivity. Only buy from the official store or authorized resellers. Never buy a used or pre-set-up device.

Bottom line

Ledger is a well-known, widely used option in its category. Whether it fits depends on the "good for / skip it if" lists above. Try the free tier (where there is one) before paying for anything.
